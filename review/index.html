How review works, and what is recorded

The pipeline reads each accident investigation report and proposes what it found. CHIRP's reviewers confirm, correct or reject each proposal and sign the report off. This page explains that workflow and the record it leaves behind: who may do what, what counts as a decision, when a report becomes verified, what the pipeline may and may not do to a reviewed report and what the audit trail holds. The step-by-step guide to the tool's screens is the reviewer guide that accompanies it.

Who does what

Every account holds one of four roles. A role is read afresh on every request, so a change to someone's role or a deactivated account takes effect at once, for their password sign-in and for any automated key they hold.

Role Can
Reader Read the documents their account allows. No decisions, no edits, no sign-off, no history. A reader's account can be limited to particular jurisdictions, or to verified documents only; a document outside that scope does not exist for them, in searches or by direct address.
Reviewer Everything a reader can, plus deciding, correcting and flagging every item, reading each item's history and signing a document off.
Analyst Everything a reviewer can, plus ingesting reports and running or re-running the pipeline, through the system's interface rather than the review tool.
Administrator Everything, plus returning a verified document to extracted and forcing a re-run or deletion over a reviewed document. Accounts and invitations are managed by Tandem for now.

What gets reviewed

Review covers the pipeline's inferences, never the text of the report itself. The sentences are the evidence everything else is read from, and they are not items. Seven kinds of item are:

Item What it is
Document fields Five: title, publication date, publication number, document type and jurisdiction (the body that published the report).
Accident particulars Eleven facts the report states about the accident: date, location, severity, accident type, loss of life, injury count, whether the vessel sank, total loss, pollution, property damage and severe environmental damage.
Incident link The match between the report and the investigators' own record of the accident (for the UK, the MAIB Data Portal), made by publication number.
Safety issues Each hazard the report identifies, kept as the sentences it spans.
Recommendations Each action the report calls for, kept as the sentences it spans, with its reference number and addressee as worded.
Entities Each vessel, person, organisation, place or thing the narrative names, with every place it is mentioned.
Addressee lists For each recommendation, the organisations it is directed at, reviewed as one item separate from the recommendation itself.

The workflow

1
Pick from the queue

The queue lists every report the pipeline has finished with, how many of its items stand in each state and who is working on it. Filter to what is flagged, what is still unreviewed or what is yours.

2
Open it, and claim it

Opening an unclaimed report puts your name on it. A claim is a note, never a lock: it tells colleagues who is working where, and lapses after 24 hours without a change.

3
Decide, or correct

Each item is accepted as it stands, corrected or rejected. A correction keeps the machine's original one step back in the history. An optional reason can go with any of the three.

4
Flag what you are unsure of

A flag with a note asks for a second pair of eyes. A flagged item stops the report being signed off until someone resolves it.

5
Sign off

A named reviewer attests that the report is fit to serve to readers. The sign-off records who, when and how many items stood in each state.

If needed
Return

An administrator can return a verified report to extracted, with a note. It leaves every verified-only reader's view at once; the item decisions are untouched.

The five states

Every item is in exactly one state at any time. An item can be decided any number of times; the latest decision is its state and every earlier one is in its history.

State Meaning
unreviewed Nobody has looked at it. The pipeline's value stands, unconfirmed. Every item starts here.
accepted A reviewer confirmed the pipeline's value as it is.
corrected A reviewer changed it. The value shown is the reviewer's; the pipeline's original is in the history. A field a reviewer has edited is marked as a reviewer edit, which outranks every source the pipeline reads from, so a later pipeline run keeps it.
rejected A reviewer said this item should not exist: a recommendation that is not one, an entity that is noise. It drops out of what readers of CHIRPdb see, but it is not deleted; a reviewer can still find it and reverse the decision.
flagged A reviewer is unsure and wrote a note saying why. The note shows on the item until it is decided again, and the report cannot be signed off while any item is flagged.

What a correction can be

  • A field or particular: a new value, chosen from the allowed list where one applies (document type, jurisdiction, severity, accident type) or typed where it does not. Title and jurisdiction can be changed but never emptied.
  • A safety issue or recommendation: its own fields (name, kind, contributory, addressed; reference number, addressee, action), or the sentences it spans. A record must span at least one sentence.
  • An entity: its name or type; merging another entity into it; moving a mention to another entity or removing one. An entity left with no mention is removed, and the tool says so before you confirm.
  • The incident link: confirmed, or cleared. A cleared link is remembered as a person's decision, so a later linking run does not put it back.
  • An addressee list: an organisation attached from the shared register, or detached. Other recommendations directed at the same organisation are untouched.

Two bulk actions exist for the long tail of entities: accept every unreviewed entity on the page, or on the whole report. Each entity accepted is its own decision with its own history entry, and an entity someone else changed in the meantime is skipped and named.

Two people on one item

Every change carries the moment the reviewer last read the item. If the item has changed since, the change is refused, and the page redraws the item showing what is stored now, including the other person's decision. Nothing of the refused change is saved. This is enforced by the database on every item write, so it holds whether the change comes from the tool or from an automated client, and a claim is never needed to make it safe.

Sign-off

Extracted → Verified → Extracted (returned)

Verified is an attestation: a named reviewer says the report is fit to serve to readers. It is not a sum of item states, for two reasons. The most entity-dense report in the corpus holds over three and a half thousand entities, so a rule that every item must be decided would make verified unreachable for exactly the reports readers most want; and a rule that signing off silently accepts every remaining item would put a reviewer's name on thousands of decisions they never made.

Sign-off is refused, naming what stands in the way, while either holds:

  • any item on the report is flagged;
  • any of the seventeen document-level items is undecided: the five fields, the eleven accident particulars or the incident link. These are few, high value and what a reader searches on.

Safety issues, recommendations, entities and addressee lists may stay unreviewed. The sign-off records how many did: for each kind of item, a count in each state as it stood at that moment, readable afterwards and unchanged by later review. Signing off again after further corrections replaces the earlier sign-off's name, time and counts.

Returning a verified report to extracted is an administrator's act and needs a note. It takes the report out of every verified-only reader's view at once, which is why it is kept apart from a reviewer's decisions. The note and the administrator's name stay on the report until it is signed off again.

The pipeline and your decisions

Every pipeline stage replaces its whole output when it runs, and deleting a report removes everything under it. Either would discard a reviewer's work, so the system refuses both for any report holding a decision (a flag counts) until an administrator forces it with a reason, which is recorded.

  • A forced re-run keeps every decided item and replaces only the unreviewed ones. A decided safety issue or recommendation stays, matched by the sentences it spans; a decided entity stays under its human name and state; an edited field keeps its value; a confirmed or cleared incident link is left alone.
  • Extraction is never re-run over a decision, forced or not. Its output is the sentences every decision is anchored to, so nothing could carry a decision across.
  • A verified report is never re-run or deleted, forced or not. The sign-off attests to what is stored. An administrator returns it to extracted first.
  • The review tool cannot trigger a re-run or a deletion. It shows each stage's status read-only; the pipeline is run through the system's own interface by an analyst or administrator.

The audit trail

The audit trail is a separate part of the database whose only job is to be true about the past. Where every other table describes what is currently the case and is free to be corrected, the trail only ever gains entries. It answers three questions about any item: what did the machine originally produce, who changed it and when, and what did they change it to.

One entry per change to one item

Every entry carries:

  • who: the account that made the change, and, where an automated key was used, the key's label;
  • when: stamped by the database, never by the caller;
  • what: the action (accept, reject, flag, correct, merge, move a mention, confirm or clear the incident link, sign off, return, forced re-run, forced delete, create, delete) and the report the item belongs to;
  • why: the reason the reviewer gave, if any;
  • before and after: the item's whole value on each side, in the live column's own type. A pair that reads the same was not changed by that write; a created item has no before and a deleted one no after.
Recommendation 2024/118 in report 9/2024 correct · by R. Hartley · Wed 14 Oct 2026, 14:32 reason: "The report numbers this 2024/118, not 2024/113."
fieldbeforeafter reference_number2024/1132024/118 addresseeThe Maritime and Coastguard AgencyThe Maritime and Coastguard Agency review_stateunreviewedcorrected

The pipeline's own writes are not entries: the trail records people. The first entry on any item therefore carries the machine's original value on its before side, which is how the tool can show Machine original at the bottom of every item's history without storing it anywhere else. A change that touches several items writes one entry each: a merge of two entities records the entity that went, the one that survived and every mention that moved.

What else is recorded

  • Sign-ins, and refused sign-ins. A refused sign-in keeps the address as it was typed, whether or not an account holds it.
  • Automated keys issued and revoked, and role changes, with the administrator who made them.
  • Account deletions, keeping the name the account held, so the trail can still say who made an older change once the account is gone.
  • Forced re-runs and deletions, with the administrator's reason, written before the rows go.

What is not recorded

Bookkeeping that asserts nothing about the corpus stays outside the trail: who is looking at a report (claims), which pipeline stage ran when and what it cost and the search fingerprints. A few less central edits are also unrecorded today: marking a recommendation implemented, SHIELD code taggings, analysis notes, legislation links and edits to the shared organisation, legislation and author registers.

Properties of the trail

Never edited, never deleted

No account the application can use holds the right to change or remove an entry, and the database checks that on every rebuild. The only way an entry comes to exist is inside the same transaction as the change it describes, so a change and its record commit together or not at all. There is no unrecorded path.

It outlives what it describes

Entries are not tied to the live rows by database constraints. A deleted report's entries are still readable under its identifier; a deleted account's entries still name it, from the name kept at deletion.

Readable by reviewers, in context

In the tool, a grey line beneath each item names who last changed it and when, and History unfolds the item's whole trail, newest first, read fresh each time so it includes what a colleague did a moment ago. Readers see none of this; the history needs the reviewer role or above. The same reads are available to automated clients with that role.

The audit schema

One table holds what every entry has in common; one table per kind of item holds that kind's before-and-after values, keyed by the entry. Scroll to pan, or hold Ctrl/Cmd and scroll to zoom.
